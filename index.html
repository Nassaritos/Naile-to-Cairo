Turkishis the scarce skill here — not English

Two things that shape everything below

1. The permit reality. A foreign national needs an employer-sponsored work permit in Egypt, and companies face quota rules limiting foreign staff relative to Egyptian staff. That means small local shops are slow to hire her, while large multinationals with Cairo service hubs (Capgemini, Agoda, Teleperformance, Nestlé) already run this process routinely — and freelance/remote work paid by clients outside Egypt sidesteps the question. Prioritise big multinationals and remote work; treat small Egyptian SMEs as a bonus.

2. Her no-Arabic gap is smaller than it looks. Cairo is one of the world's cheapest multilingual service hubs — global companies staff Turkish, French, German and Italian desks there precisely because the language is rare locally. In those roles Arabic is irrelevant and Turkish is the whole reason they'd hire her. That is the single highest-probability lane on this page.

E · What I'd actually do, in order

  1. Today — apply to the three that need no preparation. The Maadi Turkish Translator post (a day old, so she's early), the TP Egypt Turkish CSR standing vacancy, and Agoda's Cairo MEA account manager seat. Different tiers on purpose: one is a real fit, one is a fast safety net, one is the good outcome.
  2. This week — split the CV in two. One version leads with translation & localisation, one leads with Turkish-market sales & account management. A single CV listing seven different careers reads as unfocused to every recruiter on this page; two focused ones read as two strong candidates.
  3. This week — set up the income floor that doesn't need anyone's permission. Register on Smartcat and ProZ as an EN→TR translator, sign up to one AI-training platform, and put up an italki profile teaching Turkish. Three or four hours of setup, and it earns in foreign currency while the employed search runs.
  4. Next week — write the four institutional emails. Yunus Emre, the Embassy's commercial counsellor, DEİK's Türkiye–Egypt council, and two Turkish manufacturers. In Turkish, short, CV attached, naming what she'd do for them. These have no application form, so almost nobody sends them — which is exactly why they work.
  5. Ongoing — build toward the Turkish-research lane. The OSINT / media-monitoring / ICRC-researcher track is the highest-ceiling work here and the best match for her news-research and international-relations experience. It usually wants a visible track record, so: a public writing sample or two analysing Turkish politics in English, and a LinkedIn that says Turkey analyst rather than generalist.
  6. Check before signing anything — the permit. Confirm which side sponsors the work permit and how long it takes, in writing, before accepting an offer. Multinationals handle it as routine; small firms sometimes promise and stall. Freelance income from clients outside Egypt is a different question and worth checking separately with a local accountant.
